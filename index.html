A scientific live benchmark

Do we still need real human data — or can AI simulate human behavior?

AI agents, matched to specific real participants, are put through published behavioral economics experiments. Same person, same screens, same decisions. We measure how close the simulated behavior gets to what the humans actually did.

A project of the NOMIS Center for the Cognitive Foundations of Economics, University of Zurich.

Explainer
How to read the fidelity score

Imagine 100 people each picking one of five options (A through E). The red bars show how humans chose. We then ask an AI agent — matched to each person — to do the same thing, and get the blue bars. Fidelity (F) scores how close the blue is to the red.

Small gap · F = 0.80
AI picks almost like humans did.
Large gap · F = 0.15
AI picks very differently.
F = 1 AI's choices are identical to the humans'
F = 0 AI's choices are no closer than random guessing
F < 0 AI's choices are further from humans than random
The stakes

If the gap closes, a lot of things stop needing real humans.

Behavioral research, product testing, policy piloting — they all currently rely on expensive, slow human data collection. If AI can simulate people well enough, parts of that stop being necessary. We track how close we are.

Researchers

Pilot experiments and sanity-check designs before running expensive studies on real humans.

Companies

Test how actual customers would respond to a new product, price, or interface — before launching.

Governments

Estimate how citizens react to a policy change, a tax, or a benefit schedule before rollout.

The question isn't whether AI has interesting opinions. It's whether AI, given everything we know about a specific person, can produce a realistic version of what that person would do. That's what this benchmark measures.

How we measure the gap

Published experiments are the easiest possible case.

Inside a published experiment we know exactly who the person was, what they saw, and what they decided. That makes this an upper bound on how well simulation can work today. If the gap doesn't close here, it won't close in the wild.

One person, one agent

Each AI agent carries the same demographics as a real participant.

Same screens

The agent sees the exact stimulus — not a summary.

Real papers

Top-five journals: AER, QJE, JPE, REStud, Econometrica.

Two measurements

Fidelity: distribution match. Conclusion match: same finding.

Many models

Same pipeline across frontier AI. We report the approach, not a shootout.
