Results

Where the simulation approach works — and where it fails.

Each paper gets two scores: how closely AI-agent decisions match the human distribution (fidelity), and whether the paper's scientific finding survives when its tests are rerun on AI data (conclusion match). Below is what that looks like right now.

The big picture

Distribution fit vs. scientific agreement.

One dot per paper, using the best result the approach produces on that paper. Top-right papers are where the simulation works — both in distribution and in conclusion. Bottom-left is where it fails on both fronts.

Is agentization helping?

Individual matching vs. population-only matching.

The whole point of building one AI agent per specific human is that the simulation can get that specific person right — not just the average. The gap between F_individual (per-subject) and F_population (distribution-only) tells us whether matching each real person with their own AI agent is earning its keep.

Does scale help?

What if we simulate more AI agents per person?

The default run matches the original sample size 1:1. But what happens as we scale up the number of AI draws per person — 2×, 5×, 10× the human sample? Does the gap shrink, or plateau?
