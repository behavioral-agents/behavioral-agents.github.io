The two metrics

Two different questions. Both matter.

Metric 1 · Fidelity

Do the AI's decisions look like the humans'?

Fidelity is a compass: 0 = random, 1 = perfect match. Negative means worse than random.

F = 1 − EMD(AI, human) / EMD(random, human), where EMD is the Wasserstein-1 distance.

Metric 2 · Conclusion match

Would a researcher reach the same finding?

We rerun the paper's own tests on AI data. Same direction + same significance = the conclusion is reproduced.

Classifications: correct-significant, correct-direction, null, wrong.

The full pipeline

From published paper to benchmark number.

Seven steps, run the same way for every paper. Every artifact on GitHub. Any paper rerun with one command.

Pick the paper

From AER, QJE, JPE, REStud, Econometrica — enough public material to see exactly what participants saw.

Digitize the experiment

Instructions, decision screens, conditions — converted to machine-readable form. Visuals kept intact.

Match the sample

Each AI agent carries the original study's demographics. Sample size matches the human study.

Run the simulation

Via edsl, each agent walks the same survey flow as the humans. Across multiple frontier AI models.

Compare distributions

Measure how close AI decisions are to human decisions. Gives us fidelity.

Rerun the paper's own tests

The authors' regressions and hypothesis tests, applied to AI data. Same direction + significance = conclusion match.

Publish, open-source, iterate

Every reproduction package lives on public GitHub. Rerun any result with one command.

An important distinction

Not "is AI biased?" but "can AI imitate a specific human?"

There's a much-discussed question about whether AI has biases, whose views it defaults to, or how neutral it seems. That's not our question.

Our question is the other one: given everything we know about a particular person and the choice they face, can AI produce a realistic version of what that person would do? The AI isn't expressing its own views — it's being asked to imitate someone specific.

That's why the experimental economics literature is such a clean setting. These are studies where researchers had to document exactly who participated, what they were told, and what options they had. No ambiguity about what success would look like.
